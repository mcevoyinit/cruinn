The problem

A recording is not a usable record.

An interview is captured, and there the recording stops. Turning it into a record fit for disclosure and for court is a separate problem, and one that tends to be solved only when a case forces it.

Recorded by law

under statutory recording rules

Formal proceedings have been electronically recorded for decades. Turning those recordings into a written record a case can rely on is a separate job, and a much rarer one.

Commissioned case by case

priced per job, returned in days

A transcript is bought one recording at a time, and waited on. Cost and delay end up deciding which recordings become records and which stay on a disc.

No proof it is unaltered

in an ordinary transcript

A plain document shows the words but not their integrity. Nothing in it demonstrates that a line was not quietly changed after the fact.

There is also a clock. Written records are almost never wanted for their own sake. They are wanted because something downstream is waiting on them, and the people waiting are working to statutory timetables they do not control. Cost and turnaround are therefore not commercial details. They decide which recordings become records at all.

The standard

The seal is not our idea. It is already the law's.

Where the law takes a recording seriously, it does not ask anyone to trust that it is unaltered. It seals it. Under Ireland's statutory recording rules, one recording is sealed and labelled at the point of creation, given an identifying number, and signed there and then by the people present. The sealed copy is never worked from. A separate working copy is. Cruinn does the same thing for the written record that comes out of it.

What the regulations already do

The seal is applied at the moment of recording, in the presence of the person it concerns, with two signatures on the label. From that point the sealed master is the authoritative copy of what was recorded, and ordinary work is done from a working copy. Tamper evidence is built into the birth of the record, not added later.

What Cruinn extends

A transcript has never carried a seal of its own. Cruinn gives it one: the certified text is bound to a cryptographic proof, every step from receipt to delivery is written to an append only log, and the record is closed once it is certified. Any later change is evident to anyone who checks, offline, without us.

To be exact about the limits. Cruinn is not a party to the statutory sealing of a recording and does not replace it. The recording remains the record of what was said. Cruinn produces a certified, readable form of it and applies the same logic, a seal that makes any later alteration plain, to that.

Service lines

Three records, one standard

The interview is where we started, because it is where the evidential bar is highest. The same pipeline, certified by a person and sealed on delivery, applies to the other records that get made by hand and typed up afterwards. We say plainly which of these is in service today and which is not.

In service

Recorded interviews

A recorded interview returns as a certified, line numbered transcript with speakers attributed, sealed so that any later change is evident. This is the service the rest of this page describes.

In development

Written statements

Statements are still taken by hand and typed up afterwards, twice over, by the person who took them. Cruinn takes either a dictated reading or a photograph of the page and returns the text in the standard template. Anything the system is not sure of is flagged for the writer to confirm rather than quietly guessed, and the typed version is only ever a draft until a person signs it off.

Roadmap

Interpreted interviews

Where an interview runs through an interpreter, the English that the interpreter gives is what ends up in the written record, and the original language is heard once and gone. Cruinn intends to keep both: the English record, and an aligned audit track in the original language, so the two can be checked against each other later.

The framework

Built against standards already published

The organisations that run these interviews already publish the standards they hold themselves to: a decision making model with human rights and a code of ethics at its centre, and nine standards of conduct beneath it. Cruinn is not a new set of values to adopt. It is infrastructure for the ones already written down, and it is designed against them deliberately.

Gather information and intelligence
The first stage of the model ends on a plain instruction: accurate note taking and record keeping. An interview is the rawest information gathered, and a certified transcript is that instruction carried out on the hardest material there is.
Take action and review
The last stage asks for the rationale to be evidenced and stood over afterwards. A sealed record lets a decision taken after an interview be explained years later from something that cannot have moved in the meantime.
Honesty and integrity
Nothing is overwritten. Every correction made before certification is a new version with a time and an author against it, and the record is closed once it is sealed. Integrity of the record is demonstrable rather than asserted.
Transparency and communication
The certified transcript carries its own verification page, so what it says and whether it has moved are both open to the court, to the defence and to oversight, without anyone taking our word for it.
Information and privacy
Recordings and transcripts are processed and held within Ireland, on infrastructure we control, and no audio is sent to an outside service. The rights of everyone who appears in a recording are handled as the sensitive information they are.
Authority and responsibility
A provable chain of custody, from receipt to delivery, means every statement drawn from a record is attributable, and the question the model trains people to ask, whether an action can be explained in public, has an answer that does not rest on memory.

To be clear about what this is. Designing against published standards is a commitment we have made about how Cruinn is built. It is not an endorsement, an approval, or a relationship with any organisation, and none should be read into it.

What the certified record carries

The job of the record is not to be read once. It is to be handed over, quoted from, and relied on by people who were not in the room. So it is built as a disclosure exhibit rather than a text file. Below is what is in the record today, and what is being built into it next.

  • In the record Line numbered pages with a header block, so any line can be cited exactly
  • In the record Speakers attributed throughout, with a certified reading of every line
  • In the record An append only history of every correction, timestamped and attributed, with nothing overwritten
  • In the record The seal, and a verification page that anyone can check offline
  • In development Key moment anchors, marking where the caution was administered, where the allegation was put, and the responses given
  • In development An A to Z concordance index, listing each term with the page and line where it appears

Common questions

Questions, answered plainly

Where is the data processed and stored?

Within Ireland, always. Recordings and transcripts are processed and stored on infrastructure under Irish jurisdiction. No audio is sent to an outside service or a third party cloud.

Does software decide what the record says?

No. A sovereign system on our own infrastructure produces a fast first draft, but it certifies nothing. A vetted reviewer checks and certifies every line against the audio, so the certified text is the reviewer's judgement, not the software's.

What happens if a transcript is altered later?

The seal stops verifying. Alter a single character of a sealed transcript and its cryptographic seal no longer matches, so the change is evident to anyone who checks, whoever made it.

Is this only for criminal interviews?

No. Nothing in the seal is specific to one kind of interview, and the same service applies wherever a recording must stay private and provable, including tribunals of inquiry, statutory regulators and disciplinary panels. The service is built for interviews conducted under statutory recording rules, and that is what we sell today.

Can the seal be verified without Cruinn?

Yes, independently and offline. Verification checks the certified text against its sealed manifest using published methods, so it does not depend on contacting us or on us remaining in business.

Does Cruinn touch the sealed recording?

No. The sealed master and the statutory process that produced it are none of our business and are left exactly as they are. We work from a copy, and what we return is a transcript carrying a seal of its own. The recording remains the record of what was said.

What is not built yet?

Interview transcription is the service. Statement digitisation is in development and interpreted dual track recording is on the roadmap, both marked as such above, alongside the key moment anchors and the concordance index. Formal certifications are in progress and are listed by their real status. We would rather be dull about what exists than have a buyer discover the gap later.

How does this fit disclosure obligations?

Certified copies are issued for disclosure and for court, each carrying the seal, and any party that receives one can check it. Cruinn supplies the certified transcript and its proof; how it is used in a given case remains a matter for those conducting it.
